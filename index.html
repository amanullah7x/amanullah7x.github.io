Send meaning, not pixels

When drones share video over a lossy, long-delay link, most of the bandwidth goes to pixels the ground station never needs. My first-author paper measures what happens when the drone sends what it detected instead.

  • Sense1080p camera frame
  • DetectYOLOv8s on board
  • TrackByteTrack IDs
  • Encode72-byte message
  • Link150 ms, 0–30% loss
  • ReceiveGround station
Submitted · ICECT 2027, NUML First author 6 pages · IEEE format

Semantic Communication in FANETs: Edge AI for Bandwidth-Efficient UAV Swarms

A. Naseer, A. Khalid (HITEC University)

The drone runs YOLOv8s and ByteTrack on board and sends one 72-byte message per tracked target. I compared this with sending video over the same emulated flying ad-hoc network. PX4 flights in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, cross-checked in Gazebo, feed unmodified ROS 2 nodes. These talk across an NS-3 link with 150 ms delay, 50 Mb/s and 0–30% packet loss.

  • The 72-byte messages lost almost nothing beyond what the link itself dropped (0.70 delivered at 30% loss, where 0.70 is the best possible), with latency fixed near 151 ms and about 1 kB/s of traffic.
  • Best-effort 1080p JPEG delivered every frame on a clean link but none from 5% loss on. A frame is split into many RTPS fragments, and losing any one of them drops the whole frame.
  • Reliable 360p JPEG kept frames arriving, but its latency rose to 508–614 ms and it used 218–1,310× more bandwidth than the semantic stream.
  • Gazebo reproduced every trend on the same code.

Open sourceSynthetic data · Sim-to-real

Does synthetic aerial data help a real drone detector?

I built an Omniverse Replicator generator that renders labelled drone-view images of cars and people. It randomizes altitude (8–120 m), camera pitch, field of view, sun, paint and ground, and uses PhysX ray casts to place only visible objects. I then scored four YOLOv8s models on the 548 real VisDrone2019 validation images: a public VisDrone baseline and three variants I trained (real-only, real + synthetic and synthetic-only).

RF-DETR Nano for aerial vehicle-component detection

I fine-tuned RF-DETR Nano through Roboflow on a custom-annotated aerial dataset of 1,781 images at 384×384, trained for 50 epochs. The model detects ground vehicles and their parts in five classes, not just whole-vehicle boxes. The demo runs it on licensed stock aerial footage to check scale and low-contrast behaviour.

Autonomous visual tracking in an Isaac Sim digital twin

A software-in-the-loop platform that couples Isaac Sim with PX4 firmware and offboard ROS 2 nodes. A perception node finds the target, and a PID visual-servoing node turns the pixel offset into MAVLink offboard velocity setpoints. This lets me check tracking behaviour before the drone ever leaves the ground.

Autonomous subject following

A pilot-in-the-loop follow system on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Coral Edge TPU (SSD-MobileNetV2, 24–30 FPS). The pilot picks the subject with an RC switch. Kalman-filtered detections then drive MAVLink yaw and velocity commands to hold a 5 m standoff, and the drone hovers if the subject is lost.

Multi-threaded ground control station

A PyQt6 ground station with separate threads for the MAVLink link, the RTSP video and an offline Leaflet map. YOLOv8n runs in a worker thread on the live feed. Waypoint missions planned on the offline map were flown on a real drone in Guided mode.

LiDAR mapping without GPS

A mapping pipeline aimed at navigation where GPS is degraded. It uses an RPLIDAR S2 with ROS 2 Humble and builds occupancy grids in Gazebo and RViz for waypoint navigation. It was tested on the bench, in simulation and on a small ground robot.
